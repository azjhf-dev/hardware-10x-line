Ruy's pricing philosophy: a seat of Ansys goes for around $50,000 while OpenFOAM costs nothing, because a failure is so expensive that best-of-breed wins. So build the most valuable thing imaginable and charge as much as you can until customers push back. Price from value created (time saved, productivity), not from cost-plus. Boeing does not care about $100 vs $200.

Why it matters: The single most quotable economics lesson in the video - and a live one, because it explains who can afford to build in this space.

The big picture

  1. Machines got 10x cheaper. Desktop CNC with tool changers ($2,999), metal printing at micron scale, CT scanning for a few thousand a month. The garage now does what needed a factory and a bank loan.
  2. AI is arriving 3 years late to hardware - which is the opportunity. Simulation and PLM are agent-ready today; CAD is the hard frontier. Only ~10 startups are seriously building here while CEOs mandate "AI in everything."
  3. Capital just showed up. Misumi Ventures' $50M fund ($500k-$1.5M checks) exists precisely for frontier hardware, robotics and industrial AI. Cheap machines + arriving intelligence + fresh capital = the window the video is pointing at.
